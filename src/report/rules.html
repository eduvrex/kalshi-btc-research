Desk Rules

Signal RoomPaper DeskRules

Every strategy the paper engine runs: its hypothesis, its exact trigger, how fills are simulated, and the mathematics. All parameters are hypotheses under test, not calibrated truths. Everything is simulated — no real orders exist anywhere in this system.

Shared machinery

Analytic fair value (BTC). Settlement is the mean of the index over the final \(D=60\) s. For a driftless diffusion with per-second volatility \(\sigma\), seen \(\tau\) seconds from close:

$$\tau_{\text{eff}}=\tau-\tfrac{2D}{3},\qquad z=\frac{\ln(S_t/K)}{\hat\sigma\sqrt{\tau_{\text{eff}}}},\qquad \text{fair}=\Phi(z),\qquad \hat\sigma^2=\frac{1}{n}\sum_i \frac{(\Delta\ln p_i)^2}{\Delta t_i}\ \text{(trailing 30 min)}$$

Anchored fair value (alts). Each alt's Pyth feed carries a level basis vs the CF settlement index — but the strike \(K\) is the official index at window open, so anchoring to the feed's own 60-second pre-open average \(A\) cancels the basis exactly:

$$z_{\text{alt}}=\frac{\ln(P_t/A)}{\hat\sigma_{\text{alt}}\sqrt{\tau_{\text{eff}}}},\qquad A=\frac{1}{60}\sum_{s=o-60}^{o-1}P_s$$

Kalshi taker fee (makers pay zero): \( \text{fee}=\lceil 7\,C\,P(1-P)\rceil\ \text{cents per order} \).

Sizing & risk, per strategy book ($100 each): stake \(=\min(\$5,\ 5\%\times\text{book cash})\); max 6 open positions; no entries in the final 120 s; entries stop below $70 book equity or if .paper-stop exists; every position holds to settlement (P&L \(=\) contracts \(\times\mathbf 1[\text{win}]-\) cost).

taker · BTC · tests the dislocation thesis

fade — take the cheap side when model and market disagree

Hypothesis: the book sometimes lags the index by whole seconds (the project's founding observation). Trigger: \(|\Phi(z)-\text{mid}|\ge 0.05\) sustained for 3 consecutive seconds. Action: buy the model-favored side at the live best ask (YES) or \(1-\)bid (NO), taker fee included. Prediction from research: ≈flat-to-negative — three prior studies found the quote efficient at coarse granularity; this tests the seconds-scale version.

taker · BTC · controlled experiment vs fade

mcfade — same trigger, Monte Carlo fair value

Identical to fade in every respect except the model: fair value comes from a block bootstrap of the trailing 30 minutes of de-meaned empirical 1-second returns (fat tails and clustering preserved, drift removed — a fair value is a martingale quantity), \(N=1500\) paths, each scoring the exact 60-second settlement average:

$$\widehat P_{\mathrm{MC}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathbf 1\!\left[\tfrac1D\textstyle\sum_{s=c-D}^{c-1}S^{(j)}_s\ge K\right]$$

Any performance difference vs fade is attributable purely to the model, since triggers, sizing, and fills are identical.

maker · BTC · tests zero-fee spread capture

maker — rest bids behind the fair/mid blend

Hypothesis: maker fills pay no fee on these series, and every negative research result came from taker costs — resting orders may harvest the spread. Quotes:

$$b_{\text{YES}}=\big\lfloor(\min(\Phi(z),\text{mid})-0.03)\big\rfloor_{0.01},\qquad b_{\text{NO}}=\big\lfloor(\min(1-\Phi(z),1-\text{mid})-0.03)\big\rfloor_{0.01}$$

A side is not quoted at all if its raw value is under 2¢ (near-certain markets otherwise become adverse-selection donations — audit finding). Fill rule (conservative): a resting order fills only when a real captured trade prints strictly through its price — touches never fill, because queue priority is unknowable. Quotes cancel in the final 120 s.

taker · 6 alt coins · tests the lead-lag theory

lag — chase alt contracts after a dramatic BTC move

Hypothesis (Eduardo's): BTC leads, alts follow with a lag, and alt contract quotes are sticky. Trigger: BTC's 2-minute move \(|\Delta_{2m}|\ge 20\) bps, where \(\Delta_{2m}=\ln(S_t/S_{t-120})\times 10^4\). Action: buy the BTC-direction side of every active alt window (ETH, SOL, XRP, DOGE, BNB, HYPE) at the ask — unless that side is already priced ≥ 80¢ (nothing left to chase). One position per alt window.

maker · 6 alt coins · control arm

altmaker — model-free quoting on alt books

Rests both sides \(0.03\) behind the alt's own mid only — no model at all. Same floor-rounding, 2¢ minimum, strictly-through fill rule, and cutoff as maker. Role: the control arm — pure spread capture with maximum adverse-selection exposure. If altmakerfv beats it, the model earns its keep; if neither survives, alt spread capture is dead.

taker · 6 alt coins · dislocation thesis on alts

altfade — fade with the anchored alt fair value

The fade rule applied to each alt: trigger \(|\Phi(z_{\text{alt}})-\text{mid}|\ge 0.05\) for 3 s using the anchored fair value above, \(\hat\sigma_{\text{alt}}\) from the alt's own trailing 30-minute Pyth returns. Alt books are thinner than BTC's — if stickiness exists anywhere at seconds scale, it should show here first.

maker · 6 alt coins · treatment arm

altmakerfv — alt quoting with the model in the blend

Identical to altmaker except quotes blend the anchored fair value: \(b_{\text{YES}}=\lfloor\min(\Phi(z_{\text{alt}}),\text{mid})-0.03\rfloor\), and symmetrically for NO. The blend pulls quotes away from the market on the side the model distrusts — the A/B against altmaker isolates exactly what the model contributes to spread capture.

How verdicts are reached

Each book's settled P&L feeds the Monte Carlo panel on the Paper Desk: a 2,000-resample bootstrap gives the 90% CI on EV/trade, and 2,000 simulated fourteen-day futures (drawing from the strategy's own P&L history at its observed trade rate) give P(survive the $70 stop) and P(end profitable). A strategy graduates only if it survives ≥2 weeks with a positive-EV confidence interval — and even then, the next step is a conversation, not an order.
