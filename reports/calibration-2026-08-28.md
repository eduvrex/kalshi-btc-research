## How to read this

- **Multiple comparisons.** 158 cells were tested. At the conventional |z| > 1.96 threshold roughly 7.9 cells would look "significant" by pure chance, which is why the adjusted threshold |z| > 3.60 is used and marked with **.
- **Independence is violated.** Consecutive candles within one market share the same outcome, so effective sample size is smaller than n and the z-scores are optimistic. Treat them as a ranking device, not a p-value. A proper test would cluster standard errors by market; the reversal panel is the cleanest cell because it draws at most a few observations per market.
- **Survivorship and selection.** Only settled markets with a two-sided quote appear. Windows that never developed a real market are excluded, which biases toward the liquid regime — the opposite of the thin-market regime under investigation.
- **This test cannot see sub-minute dislocations.** It uses 1-minute candle closes. A negative result here kills the coarse thesis, not the second-scale one, which needs the live order-book collectors.
- **A flat result is a real result.** If quoted prices match realized frequencies, these markets are efficient at this granularity and the correct decision is to stop, not to search harder for a pattern.
