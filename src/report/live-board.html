The fair value
Settlement is the mean of BRTI over the final \(D=60\) s. Under a driftless diffusion with per-second volatility \(\sigma\), the variance of that mean seen from \(\tau\) seconds out is
$$\operatorname{Var}\!\left[\tfrac{1}{D}\textstyle\int_{\tau-D}^{\tau} W_s\,ds\right]=\sigma^2\!\left(\tau-D+\tfrac{D}{3}\right)=\sigma^2\,\tau_{\text{eff}},\qquad \tau_{\text{eff}}=\tau-\tfrac{2D}{3}$$
so the normalized distance and fair value are
$$z_t=\frac{\ln\!\big(S_t/K\big)}{\hat\sigma\sqrt{\tau_{\text{eff}}}}\,,\qquad \widehat{P}(\text{YES})=\Phi(z_t),\qquad \hat\sigma^2=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\big(\Delta\ln p_i\big)^2}{\Delta t_i}\ \ \text{(trailing 30 min)}$$
The Monte Carlo fair drops the GBM assumption entirely: resample the last 30 min of empirical 1-second returns in 15-second blocks (fat tails and microstructure ride along), simulate \(N=1500\) paths to the close, and score each path's exact 60-second settlement average:
$$\widehat{P}_{\mathrm{MC}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathbf 1\!\left[\tfrac{1}{D}\textstyle\sum_{s=c-D}^{c-1}S_s^{(j)}\ \ge\ K\right],\qquad \operatorname{SE}=\sqrt{\widehat{P}_{\mathrm{MC}}\big(1-\widehat{P}_{\mathrm{MC}}\big)/N}$$
When \(\widehat{P}_{\mathrm{MC}}\) and \(\Phi(z)\) disagree, the difference is the non-Gaussianity of the last half hour. Both trade side-by-side on the Paper Desk (strategies fade vs mcfade) — the desk decides which model earns its keep.

The path statistics & the similar-windows estimator
Each window's 1-second path \(p_t\) is anchored to its own pre-open minute, so venue-level basis cancels; the strike sits at the anchor by construction:
$$a=\frac{1}{60}\sum_{s=o-60}^{o-1}p_s\,,\qquad f=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=o}^{c-1}\mathbf 1\!\left[p_t>a\right],\qquad w=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=o}^{c-1}\big(p_t-a\big)$$
\(f\) is the fraction of time above the strike; \(w\) is the time-weighted average signed distance in dollars ("12 minutes at \$20 above" ⇒ \(f=0.8,\ w\approx+16\)). The live "similar windows" number is a banded nearest-neighbour estimate over \(N=6{,}322\) historical windows, evaluated at the last completed minute mark \(m\in\{3,6,9,12\}\):
$$\mathcal N=\Big\{i:\ m_i=m,\ \ |f_i-f|\le 0.12,\ \ |w_i-w|\le\max\!\big(20,\ 0.6\,|w|\big)\Big\}$$ $$\hat p=\frac{1}{|\mathcal N|}\sum_{i\in\mathcal N}\mathbf 1\big[Y_i=\text{YES}\big]\,,\qquad \operatorname{SE}\big(\hat p\big)=\sqrt{\frac{\hat p\,(1-\hat p)}{|\mathcal N|}}$$
It is a lookup, not a fitted model: no coefficients, no extrapolation — just how windows in the same state actually settled. Prefix rows use only seconds \(\le m\): strictly no look-ahead.
